Welcome to the IETF!

We recommend that everyone who wants to get started with the IETF reads our [Introduction to the IETF](https://www.ietf.org/about/introduction/) as this succinctly covers the basics of what we are, who we are, what we do and how we do it.

Once you have a basic understanding of the IETF, as almost all of the work of the IETF takes place in Working Groups, we recommend that you read our [Guide to IETF Working Groups](https://www.ietf.org/process/wgs/). 

Then to finish the initial set, we strongly recommend that you read our [Guide to being effective in the IETF}(https://www.ietf.org/participate/being-effective/).

For most people those three are the minimum to get you started, but:
* if you have some work that you want to bring to the IETF, we recommend that you read [Bringing new work to the IETF](https://www.ietf.org/process/new-work/), or
* if you are considering participating in an IETF meeting the we recommend reading the [Guide to IETF Meetings](https://www.ietf.org/meeting/guide-ietf-meetings/).
* if you are interested in reading or implementing existing RFCs then [About RFCs](https://www.ietf.org/process/rfcs/) explains the basics of the RFC Series.

If you've got this far and you're looking for more to read then we recommend our [Guide to the IETF standards process][https://www.ietf.org/process/process/] for a more detailed explanation of IETF processes.

When you're ready to join the conversation, subscribe to a [Mailing List](https://www.ietf.org/participate/lists/).

Finally, if you want to see the IETF in action, then see our [YouTube Channel](https://www.youtube.com/channel/UC8dtK9njBLdFnBahHFp0eZQ), which has over 4000 recordings of IETF working sessions, generally published within hours of a session finishing.
